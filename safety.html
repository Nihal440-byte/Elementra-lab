Chemical Safety

Good science begins with risk awareness. Understand labels, exposure routes, safe storage and the importance of reliable safety documentation.

!

Before working with a chemical

  • Read the container label and the current Safety Data Sheet (SDS).
  • Identify hazards, exposure routes, incompatibilities and required controls.
  • Use appropriate engineering controls, such as a fume hood where required.
  • Wear task-appropriate protective equipment specified by the SDS and institutional procedure.
  • Ensure emergency eyewash, safety shower and spill procedures are available where applicable.
⌂

Storage & handling principles

  • Keep chemicals in compatible, clearly labelled, closed containers.
  • Separate incompatible classes according to institutional guidance.
  • Do not store chemicals in food or drink containers.
  • Keep flammables away from ignition sources and oxidizers.
  • Never mix unknown substances or improvise chemical experiments.
↗

Understand exposure routes

Common routes include inhalation, skin or eye contact, ingestion and injection. The level of risk depends on the substance, dose, concentration, duration and conditions of exposure.

♻

Waste & environmental care

Never pour unknown or hazardous chemicals into sinks, drains or the environment. Follow your institution's waste classification, collection and disposal procedure.

Important: This page is general educational information, not a substitute for laboratory training, a chemical-specific SDS, local regulations or your institution's approved safety procedures. In an exposure or emergency, follow the site emergency plan and contact qualified emergency responders.
