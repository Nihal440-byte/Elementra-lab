UNDERSTANDING THE ELEMENTS · 03

Element Properties

Learn how scientists describe elements through atomic structure, physical behavior, chemical reactivity and periodic patterns.

PROPERTY FRAMEWORK

What makes an element unique?

Properties are measured under defined conditions. Values can vary with temperature, pressure, allotrope and reference convention.

01

Atomic properties

Atomic properties describe the structure and energy of an atom.

  • Atomic number: number of protons in the nucleus.
  • Atomic mass: weighted average mass of naturally occurring isotopes, where applicable.
  • Electron configuration: arrangement of electrons in atomic orbitals.
  • Atomic radius: an estimated measure of atomic size, defined by a specific convention.
  • Ionization energy: energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom or ion.
02

Physical properties

Physical properties can be observed or measured without changing chemical identity.

  • State of matter: solid, liquid or gas under stated conditions.
  • Density: mass per unit volume at a specified temperature.
  • Melting point: temperature at which solid and liquid phases coexist at a given pressure.
  • Boiling point: temperature at which vapor pressure equals external pressure.
  • Electrical conductivity: ability to carry electric current.
03

Chemical properties

Chemical properties describe how an element participates in chemical change.

  • Oxidation states: formal charge assignments used to track electron transfer.
  • Reactivity: tendency to undergo chemical reactions under defined conditions.
  • Electronegativity: relative ability to attract shared electrons in a bond.
  • Common compounds: substances formed when elements combine in defined ratios.
04

Periodic trends

Patterns across periods and groups help predict elemental behavior.

  • Atomic radius: generally decreases across a period and increases down a group.
  • Ionization energy: generally increases across a period and decreases down a group.
  • Electronegativity: generally increases toward the upper-right of the table (excluding noble-gas conventions).
  • Metallic character: generally increases down a group and toward the left.
